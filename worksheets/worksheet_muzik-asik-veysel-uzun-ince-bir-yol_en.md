### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Picture a seven-year-old child in the village of Sivrialan in Sivas who lost the light of both eyes to smallpox. To comfort Veysel whose world turned pitch black, his father gifted him a modest saz to while away the hours. Nobody could have foreseen that melodies from that fragile instrument would, half a century later, become the collective conscience and philosophy of an entire nation. Deprived of outer light, Âşık Veysel uncovered an infinite luminescence within.

His verse 'I am on a long and narrow road, walking day and night' distills human existence in a single breath where heavy philosophical tomes labor for pages. For Veysel, soil is no barren mud, but the 'faithful beloved' that nurtures humanity and embraces all without expecting anything in return. Even in eras marred by discrimination and bitter polarization, he proclaimed fraternity, tolerance, and compassion through his saz. His eyes perceived no earthly sights, yet the truths perceived by his heart illuminate millions of paths to this day.
