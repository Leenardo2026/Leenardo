### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Climbing a hillside along the Aegean coast, one is greeted by the gentle rustle of olive leaves flashing silver in the breeze. For people inhabiting these lands across millennia, the olive has never been a mere agricultural crop, but the very heart of cuisine and daily life. Tenaciously enduring drought and harsh winters on barren slopes, this tree has witnessed the rise and fall of civilizations for centuries. That is why Mediterranean folk revere the olive as the 'immortal tree,' treasuring the golden oil pressed from its fruit as a sacred gift.

The culinary category known as 'zeytinyağlılar' in Turkish cuisine possesses an elegance seldom matched across world gastronomies. Seasonal vegetables like green beans, artichokes, or celeriac are simmered gently with plenty of extra virgin olive oil, a pinch of sugar, and fresh herbs, then always served chilled. Gracing tables on warm summer days, these light dishes offer the most refreshing response to the Mediterranean heat. An olive-oil dish is never a rushed meal; it is a serene dining pleasure paying quiet homage to nature's rhythm.
