### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
'Colony Collapse Disorder', which alarmed the scientific community in 2006, describes worker bees abruptly abandoning hives without returning. In particular, neonicotinoid pesticides paralyze bees' navigational senses and olfactory antenna receptors.

The collapse of bees would not solely halt agriculture; it would eradicate wild flora sustaining herbivorous wildlife. Triggering a bottom-up cascading trophic collapse, this could ignite catastrophic famines across the planet.

### 📚 Kilit Kelimeler (Key Vocabulary)
- **collapse**: collapse *(kök: çökmek - to collapse)*
- **beehive**: beehive *(kök: kovan - beehive)*
- **herbivore / herbivorous**: herbivore / herbivorous *(kök: ot - herb / grass)*
- **mass / widespread**: mass / widespread *(kök: kitle - mass / bulk)*

### ❓ Anlama ve Konuşma Soruları (Comprehension Questions)

**Dil Bilgisi Odağı:** Literary & Fluent Essay Style, Rich Adverbials & Connectors, Natural Cause & Effect
> Sophisticated C1 essay exploring What Happens If Bees Disappear? with authentic register, rich adverbial clauses, and fluent argumentation.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
There are catastrophes that unfold in silence; they arrive without deafening explosions and topple no towers, yet dislodge the cornerstones of life. The precipitous decline of the world's bee population represents precisely such a crisis. Tiny pollen grains ferried from blossom to blossom are in truth the most critical architects of our global food system. While we boast of high-tech greenhouse investments or AI-powered harvest machines, we owe one out of every three mouthfuls on market shelves to an insect with wings a few millimeters wide.

What, then, if bees were to vanish altogether? This scenario implies far more than honey disappearing from breakfast tables; it would induce an irreparable fracture in the ecosystem's backbone. Yields of hundreds of staple crops from apples to almonds, and cotton to coffee would plummet to rock bottom. Subsequently, forage fodder like alfalfa feeding livestock would wither, spreading the catastrophe directly into animal husbandry. Scenes in parts of China where farmers pollinate apple trees by hand with tiny brushes serve as a poignant warning of what might await us.
