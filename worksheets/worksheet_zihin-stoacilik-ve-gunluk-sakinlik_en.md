### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Stoicism is a philosophy of life that originated two thousand years ago. This philosophy teaches people how to remain calm in difficult times.

In life, some things are in our control, while other things are not. The weather, traffic, or other people's opinions are not in our hands. Yet our own emotions, reactions, and decisions are entirely in our hands.

Stoics do not distress over things they cannot change; they focus solely on their own actions.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), pioneered in the mid-twentieth century by Aaron Beck and Albert Ellis, draws its foundational insights directly from Stoic thought. Ellis's Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy translated Epictetus's axiom—that cognitive appraisals mediate between events and emotions—into modern clinical practice.

Modern individuals cultivate resilience by screening cognitive distortions like catastrophizing and overgeneralization through the Stoic dialectical sieve. Thus, Stoicism ceases to be a dusty classical philosophy, emerging instead as a quintessential manual of rational mental hygiene in an age of chronic anxiety.

**Dil Bilgisi Odağı:** Literary & Fluent Essay Style, Rich Adverbials & Connectors, Natural Cause & Effect
> Sophisticated C1 essay exploring Stoicism and Daily Calm with authentic register, rich adverbial clauses, and fluent argumentation.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
In our modern world where we awaken to phone notifications and tumble amid restless news feeds and worries, an ancient philosophy offers a surprisingly fresh breath: Stoicism. Spanning from Roman Emperor Marcus Aurelius to the slave-born sage Epictetus, this tradition shows how to stand resolute in life rather than spinning abstract theories. The fundamental maxim of the Stoics is remarkably clear: in life there are things within our control, and things utterly outside it.

We cannot alter the weather, what others murmur behind our backs, or gridlock on the highway. Yet the response we choose, the posture we adopt, and our inner serenity are governed solely by ourselves. As Epictetus observed, what unsettles us is not events themselves, but the premature judgments we attach to them. Hammering the steering wheel in fury during a jam will not clear the highway; it only poisons our own day. Stoicism is the art of constructing an impregnable citadel of calm within our own mind amidst the world's inevitable tempests.
