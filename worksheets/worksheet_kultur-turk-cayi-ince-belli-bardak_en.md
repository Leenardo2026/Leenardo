### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
In Turkey, tea is a beloved beverage consumed every single day. People drink tea at breakfast, at work, and at home in the evening.

Tea is prepared in a special two-tiered teapot. Water boils in the lower kettle, while loose tea leaves steep on top. Tea is always served in small, tulip-shaped glass cups.

The clear glass shows the tea's beautiful ruby color and preserves warmth.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
The design of the tulip-shaped glass, an emblem of Turkish tea culture, is a triumph of both aesthetics and functional engineering. The narrow waist prevents hot tea beneath from dissipating heat rapidly, while the flaring rim cools the liquid just enough to spare the lips.

Furthermore, thanks to clear glass, tea connoisseurs can visually evaluate the liquor's brightness and the crystal ruby clarity celebrated as 'rabbit's blood'. Fitting snugly into the palm, this contour warms cold fingers during winter, offering an intimate tactile sensation.

The loss of Yemen following the First World War rendered coffee imports—an Ottoman staple for centuries—prohibitively expensive. Through scientific initiatives led by agronomist pioneers like Zihni Derin, tea farming thrived extraordinarily within Rize's microclimate. Consequently, tea transcended being an economical alternative, evolving into the most democratic common denominator of public gatherings and civic life.

**Dil Bilgisi Odağı:** Literary & Fluent Essay Style, Rich Adverbials & Connectors, Natural Cause & Effect
> Sophisticated C1 essay exploring Turkish Tea and the Tulip Glass Culture with authentic register, rich adverbial clauses, and fluent argumentation.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Stepping into a shop in Turkey, waiting in a bank queue, or visiting a friend, the first sound you hear is almost always the same: the gentle clink of a tea spoon against porcelain. While tea in many parts of the world is a quiet refreshment savored at set hours, in Turkey it is an ongoing invitation to conversation that permeates every moment of the day. The teapot brewed at breakfast rarely cools down until evening. This drink serves as the most natural and unpretentious bond uniting diverse segments of society around the same friendly table.

The hourglass shape of the thin-waisted glass is also an integral element of this cultural experience. Its transparent glass lets one appreciate tea's clear ruby hue and freshness, while the narrow waist keeps the bottom portion pleasantly hot. Offering someone a glass of tea is the most direct way of saying, 'I have time for you, and I am ready to listen.' No matter how fast modern life becomes, a fresh glass of tea calls people to pause and speak with one another.
