### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
In shadow theatre, a single artist conducts the entire performance; this master puppeteer is honored as 'Hayali' or 'Hayalbaz'. While adeptly maneuvering the figures on wooden rods behind the screen, the Hayali mimics the distinct voices and dialects of dozens of characters unaided.

Throughout the play, characters such as the dandy Çelebi, the brawler Deli Bekir, and figures of Black Sea, Armenian, and Jewish descent reflect the empire's cosmopolitan mosaic. Thus, beneath its farcical dialogues, the screen of Karagöz encapsulates a profound ethos of societal tolerance and satire.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
Far beyond simple popular entertainment, traditional Turkish shadow theatre functioned historically as a civic safety valve voicing public critique of bureaucratic and ruling elites. Even during eras of strict palace censorship, Karagöz on the screen enjoyed an unassailable sphere of free expression through cunning innuendo and linguistic subversion.

Composed of Prologue, Dialogue, Main Interlude, and Epilogue, the dramaturgical spine of the play drew upon improvisation to remain vibrantly contemporary. This peerless poetic and anthropological wealth was universally recognized with Karagöz's 2009 inscription on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
When candlelight flickers behind a taut white calico screen, all the voices, contradictions, and laughter of Ottoman urban life come alive. As puppets carved from camel or water buffalo leather and tinted with madder pigments glide on rods, spectators shed daily worries into an enchanting atmosphere. The central conflict is crystal clear: Karagöz, voicing the blunt, outspoken populace, confronts Hacivat, who speaks an ornate tongue, observant of decorum yet shrewd. The dialogue of this duo is essentially the commoner's witty negotiation with elite officialdom.

Karagöz shadow theater was never merely child's play. On the contrary, it served as a potent civic rostrum satirizing neighbourhood grievances, corrupt officials, and social inequalities behind a shield of humor. Furthermore, behind the curtain sat a lone master artist, titled 'Hayali'—the conjurer of illusions. That solitary master animated the accents of dozens of characters across assorted ethnicities and trades in a single breath, projecting the empire's vibrant human mosaic onto the screen. When the play drew to an end, the candle was extinguished, leaving the cloth pristine and silent, while an affectionate smile lingered whispering the transience of life.
