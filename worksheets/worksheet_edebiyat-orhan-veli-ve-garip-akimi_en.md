**Dil Bilgisi Odağı:** Literary & Fluent Essay Style, Rich Adverbials & Connectors, Natural Cause & Effect

### 📖 Okuma Parçası (English)
In the early 1940s, Turkish poetry lingered under the shadow of a centuries-old weighty, ornate tradition. Poets regarded grandiloquent emotions, elaborate meters, and flamboyant metaphors as indispensable requisites of literature. Right at this juncture, a young poet roaming Ankara's avenues with notebook in hand flung the gates of literature wide open to the street: Orhan Veli Kanık. The Garip movement he launched with friends wrested poetry from palaces and salons, gifting it to the daily life of the common person.

The aching toe corn Orhan Veli introduced into verse with the line 'What a pity for Süleyman Efendi' triggered a genuine shockwave among the era's elites. For the first time, verse spoke not of kings or heroes, but in the vernacular of clerks hurrying to work with worn shoes, street vendors, and fishermen. His sentences were delightfully plain; yet behind this simplicity lay consummate linguistic mastery and profound irony. Declaring 'I listen to Istanbul, my eyes closed,' he captured not merely the vista, but the city's winds, ferry whistles, and weary bustle in his heart. The Garip movement proved that poetry is no luxury ornament, but humanity itself.
